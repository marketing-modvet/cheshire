4 · The Saturday → Sunday relief mechanism

🩻Saturday today

6.2 vets handle 66.9 appts (10.9 each — week's highest) and manage only 4.0 scans, the week's lowest. Overflow, non-urgent scans, and deferrable consults get pushed to Sunday/Monday.

➕Saturday with the new vet

Roster rises to ~7.2 (+16% capacity). At current demand that's ~9.3 appts/vet — finally in line with weekdays — plus same-day ultrasound capability all Saturday.

😌Sunday decompresses

Sunday's 90.2 appts/day partly consists of Saturday spillover. With Saturday absorbing its own demand (and the new vet also working Sunday), per-vet load drops from 10.1 toward ~9.1 — and scans stop stacking into Monday.

🎯Monday mops up

Monday is the hospital's busiest day (92.9 appts, 5.5 scans). Having the new vet's fourth day here clears any residual weekend backlog before their Tue–Thu break, when the hospital is at its quietest (78–85 appts/day).

💰 Realistic incremental estimate: AED 1.0–1.4M / year

Not every appointment the new vet sees is new money — some would have been absorbed by existing vets. But this hire is deliberately aimed at the days where the hospital is supply-constrained (Sat/Sun/Mon), where turned-away or deferred demand is real. Applying a 70–80% incrementality factor to the steady-state range gives ≈ AED 1.0–1.4M of genuinely new annual revenue.

Three upside factors not in that number:

① Imaging mix — ultrasound-heavy caseloads bill above the AED 928 hospital average (full abdomen, cardiac echo, US-guided procedures are premium items). ② Cardiac echo capacity — 73 echo studies/quarter at the hospital are currently bottlenecked on specialist availability; if the new vet is echo-competent this line can grow immediately. ③ Retention — weekend clients currently told "come back Monday" or referred elsewhere are churn risks; weekend same-day capability protects the ~AED 140k that Sat+Sun already generate per week.

Break-even check: at an all-in cost of ~AED 30–45k/month (AED 360–540k/yr), the hire pays for itself ~2.5× over even in the conservative steady-state scenario.

Methodology

Sources: ezyVet API, 20 May – 18 Aug 2026 (90 days), Dubai time. Hospital scope = ezyVet ownership/separation #4 (Modern Vet Hospital): appointments matched via the hospital's 985 resources (8,232 hospital appointments network-tagged, 7,486 with hospital vet resources used for per-vet math); 70 "Dr"-named hospital resources tracked daily for vets-working counts; 441 ultrasound-matched diagnostic requests (of 652 network-wide) by ownership code; 6,700 hospital-coded invoices totalling AED 6.95M (subtotals, ex-tax).

Scoring: per-day need = 0.40 × appts/vet + 0.30 × US/day + 0.15 × US/vet + 0.15 × staffing thinness, normalized 0–1. Rotation score = sum of ON-day scores; Saturday-weighted variant adds +0.15 to Saturday per the weekend-coverage directive. All 7 wrap-around rotations evaluated.

Caveats: ultrasound demand measured from requested diagnostics — it under-counts suppressed weekend demand (the true Saturday gap is likely larger than shown). Revenue per appointment is a blended hospital average; the incrementality factor (70–80%) is an estimate, not measured. v1 of this report used network-wide data; this v2 supersedes it. Re-run after 4–6 weeks of the new rotation to validate.
