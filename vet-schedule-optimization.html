4 · Why each recommended day earns its place

📈Friday

Highest absolute volume of the week (196 clinical appts/day) and 7.5 US requests/day. Per-vet load is moderate only because 9.7 vets are rostered — an imaging-capable vet still lifts throughput on the busiest day, and protects against Friday's high surgery count (204/quarter, week's highest).

🩻Saturday

Thinnest roster of the week (7.2 vets) yet those vets carry the highest ultrasound demand per vet (0.97/day) and 21.9 appts each. One extra hybrid vet directly fixes the weekend bottleneck.

🔥Sunday

Highest GP pressure of the entire week — 22.6 appts per vet (189/day on 8.4 vets). Ultrasound counts look low, but that's almost certainly unmet demand: scans get deferred to Monday when imaging capacity returns.

🎯Monday

The week's ultrasound peak — 8.2 requests/day (weekend backlog included) at 0.96 per vet, plus a heavy 21.5 appts/vet GP load. The single most valuable day for a sonography-capable vet.

Methodology

Sources: ezyVet API — 31,300 appointments (20 May – 18 Aug 2026), filtered to ~15,984 clinical appointments across 10 clinical types; 12,344 diagnostic requests, of which 652 matched ultrasound keywords (ultrasound, TFAST/AFAST, echocardio, sonography, US-guided). Staffing figures (scheduled vets/hours, booked hours, utilization) from the weekly scheduling report supplied 18 Aug 2026. All timestamps converted to Dubai time (UTC+4).

Scoring: per-day need = 0.40 × GP pressure (appts/vet) + 0.30 × absolute US demand + 0.15 × US demand per vet + 0.15 × staffing thinness, each normalized 0–1 across the week. Rotation score = sum of the four ON days. All 7 wrap-around rotations evaluated.

Caveats: ultrasound demand is measured from requested diagnostics — it under-counts suppressed demand on days with no sonographer (esp. Sunday). Utilization figures include all booked time, not imaging specifically. Re-run after 4–6 weeks of the new schedule to validate.
