4 · The Saturday → Sunday relief mechanism

🩻Saturday today

6.2 vets handle 66.9 appts (10.9 each — week's highest) and manage only 4.0 scans, the week's lowest. Overflow, non-urgent scans, and deferrable consults get pushed to Sunday/Monday.

➕Saturday with the new vet

Roster rises to ~7.2 (+16% capacity). At current demand that's ~9.3 appts/vet — finally in line with weekdays — plus same-day ultrasound capability all Saturday.

😌Sunday decompresses

Sunday's 90.2 appts/day partly consists of Saturday spillover. With Saturday absorbing its own demand (and the new vet also working Sunday), per-vet load drops from 10.1 toward ~9.1 — and scans stop stacking into Monday.

🎯Monday mops up

Monday is the hospital's busiest day (92.9 appts, 5.5 scans). Having the new vet's fourth day here clears any residual weekend backlog before their Tue–Thu break, when the hospital is at its quietest (78–85 appts/day).

💰 Realistic incremental estimate: AED 1.0–1.4M / year

Not every appointment the new vet sees is new money — some would have been absorbed by existing vets. But this hire is deliberately aimed at the days where the hospital is supply-constrained (Sat/Sun/Mon), where turned-away or deferred demand is real. Applying a 70–80% incrementality factor to the steady-state range gives ≈ AED 1.0–1.4M of genuinely new annual revenue.

Three upside factors not in that number:

① Imaging mix — ultrasound-heavy caseloads bill above the AED 928 hospital average (full abdomen, cardiac echo, US-guided procedures are premium items). ② Cardiac echo capacity — 73 echo studies/quarter at the hospital are currently bottlenecked on specialist availability; if the new vet is echo-competent this line can grow immediately. ③ Retention — weekend clients currently told "come back Monday" or referred elsewhere are churn risks; weekend same-day capability protects the ~AED 140k that Sat+Sun already generate per week.

Break-even check: at an all-in cost of ~AED 30–45k/month (AED 360–540k/yr), the hire pays for itself ~2.5× over even in the conservative steady-state scenario.

All 13 gap days are Saturdays, spaced exactly 28 days apart — 24 Jan, 21 Feb, 21 Mar, 18 Apr, 16 May, 13 Jun, 11 Jul, 8 Aug, 5 Sep, 3 Oct, 31 Oct, 28 Nov, 26 Dec 2026. This lines up directly with Section 1–4's finding that Saturday is already the hospital's thinnest-rostered, highest-need day — on these 13 Saturdays specifically, that thin roster also has zero in-house ultrasound capability, meaning any scan request has to wait, get referred out, or wait for an on-call vet.

🎯 How the new hybrid vet should close this gap

The recommended Fri–Sat–Sun–Mon rotation (Section 3) already covers all 13 Saturday gap dates — since the new vet works every Saturday under that plan, this single hire fully eliminates the recurring zero-coverage day without needing any change to Ivan's or Isabel's existing rotation.

Which shift should the new vet work? Not a fixed one — and specifically not a standard MID shift. Section 8 below scores every option: because Ivan and Isabel's gaps rotate between mornings, afternoons and whole days on a fixed 4-week cycle, a MID shift mostly duplicates hours that are already covered and turns out to be the worst fixed choice. A 4-week flex rotation matched to their cycle is the winner.

Don't touch the Ivan/Johny ↔ Isabel/Shakila stagger. It's already doing real work — un-staggering it (e.g. aligning both onto the same 4-on cycle) would just move the gap around rather than close it, and would also reintroduce the same-shift double-booking the team currently avoids. The new vet should be treated as a third, independent rotation layered on top, not a replacement or resync of the existing two.

🏆 Recommended: 4-week flex rotation, published months in advance

Because Ivan & Isabel's rotation repeats identically every 28 days with zero variance all year (verified: 11 sampled cycles, 16/16 shift slots matching every time), the “flex” schedule isn't ad-hoc at all — it's a fixed 4-week pattern that can be printed into the roster for the entire year on day one.

WK A (Ivan-PM Sat): AM · AM · AM · AM   │   WK B (gap Sat): PM · LONG · AM · AM   │   WK C (Isabel-AM Sat): AM · PM · PM · AM   │   WK D (full Sat): AM · AM · AM · PM   (Fri · Sat · Sun · Mon)

What it buys: demand-exposed scan hours on Fri–Mon drop from ~366 scan-slots/year (baseline) to ~52 — an 86% reduction — vs only a 29% reduction if the new vet sat on a standard MID shift (~261 still exposed). The 13 zero-coverage Saturdays disappear entirely, and every fourth Saturday needs just one long day (13×/year) to do it. If a long day isn't contractually possible, PM is the fallback on gap Saturdays (score drops 95.0% → 92.5%, still far ahead of any fixed pattern).

Methodology

Sources: ezyVet API, 20 May – 18 Aug 2026 (90 days), Dubai time. Hospital scope = ezyVet ownership/separation #4 (Modern Vet Hospital): appointments matched via the hospital's 985 resources (8,232 hospital appointments network-tagged, 7,486 with hospital vet resources used for per-vet math); 70 "Dr"-named hospital resources tracked daily for vets-working counts; 441 ultrasound-matched diagnostic requests (of 652 network-wide) by ownership code; 6,700 hospital-coded invoices totalling AED 6.95M (subtotals, ex-tax).

Scoring: per-day need = 0.40 × appts/vet + 0.30 × US/day + 0.15 × US/vet + 0.15 × staffing thinness, normalized 0–1. Rotation score = sum of ON-day scores; Saturday-weighted variant adds +0.15 to Saturday per the weekend-coverage directive. All 7 wrap-around rotations evaluated.

Caveats: ultrasound demand measured from requested diagnostics — it under-counts suppressed weekend demand (the true Saturday gap is likely larger than shown). Revenue per appointment is a blended hospital average; the incrementality factor (70–80%) is an estimate, not measured. v1 of this report used network-wide data; this v2 supersedes it. Re-run after 4–6 weeks of the new rotation to validate.

Section 7 (added 19 Aug 2026): source is the live Al Wasl staffing Google Sheet, full year 2026 (Weeks 2–52, 357 tracked days). Ivan (Team 2 vet) and Johny (Team 2 assistant) vs. Isabel (Team 4 vet) and Shakila (Team 4 assistant) tracked by parsing each week's per-team shift code (AM/PM/MID) and vet/assistant name cells. A day counts as "on" only when both a shift code and a vet name are present for that team that day (blank cells = team not scheduled that day). SICK-flagged days were left counted as "on" since the sheet still lists a shift/name for them (7 such flags across the year, ~2% of days — not material to the pattern). Gap dates verified as spaced exactly 28 days apart with zero exceptions.

Section 8 (added 19 Aug 2026): day modelled as 4 quarters; AM = Q1–Q2, MID = Q2–Q3, PM = Q3–Q4, long day = Q1–Q4 (union of quarter sets — two people on identical hours add no extra coverage). Quarters weighted by each day's ultrasound demand (Section 1 scans/day). Scores computed over the new vet's Fri–Mon days across one full 28-day cycle; the Ivan/Isabel cycle was verified identical in 11 sampled cycles across 2026 (16/16 slots each) so one cycle generalizes to the whole year. All 3 uniform fixed shifts, all 81 fixed weekly patterns, and per-slot optimal flex assignments were evaluated exhaustively (script: score_shift_options2.py). “Scan-slots exposed” = demand-weighted uncovered quarters × 13 cycles/year — demand occurring in windows with no sonographer on site, not necessarily lost revenue (some rebooks internally).
