Marketing Budget Reality Check — 2026

Setting accurate expectations on what the current marketing budget and team can and cannot deliver

Prepared by Andrew (Marketing Director)

Internal — Leadership Only

Purpose

This document sets accurate expectations with fellow leadership on what the current marketing budget and team can and cannot deliver — using real growth data, real cost comparisons, and real absorbed-value evidence — before any specific budget change is proposed. It is not a budget-increase request; that is a separate, future conversation.

Note on "unique clients": this figure nets out natural churn (pets that pass away, clients relocating out of Dubai or to Abu Dhabi) against new acquisitions, so it understates real acquisition activity. New clients acquired is the more accurate acquisition metric.

  • Unique clients grew +14.8% (2022→2025) while revenue grew +99% over the same period — most of the net growth came from AOV (+48.7%) and repeat-visit frequency.
  • New-client acquisition itself has held up reasonably well on a flat/thin paid budget: 2026 annualizes to ~8,040 new clients, +7.6% vs. 2025's 7,469 — acquisition is not collapsing, it's basically flat-to-slightly-up despite paid media never growing.
The real story is a ceiling, not a decline: acquisition has plateaued in the 7,000–9,700/year range since 2023 rather than compounding alongside the revenue/AOV growth. Holding steady on a starved budget is a credit to the team's efficiency (SEO/content-driven acquisition), but it also means the acquisition lever has not been meaningfully scaled up — the one lever that structurally requires paid reach to grow beyond this range.

AED 85,000/month in paid media split across that many distinct businesses and 11 clinics is ~AED 7,700–10,600 per business-unit or clinic if spread evenly — and in reality it isn't spread evenly; flagship brands/clinics absorb most of it, smaller/newer ones (Khawaneej, Azizi, Vienna Vet, BOVC) get very little.

There is no spare capacity in the current budget for a new channel type (offline media, signboards, lightboxes, mall displays). This isn't a prioritization failure — the money to fund it does not exist inside the current structure without cutting something that's already running.
The stronger use of any incremental budget is more paid media, not offline experiments. Offline out-of-home (signboards, lightboxes, mall displays) has weak-to-no attribution, long lead times, and fixed costs that don't scale down for a single clinic's needs. An incremental AED 70,000/month directed into existing, measurable paid channels — where results, targeting, and spend can be tracked and adjusted in real time — will outperform the same money spent on offline media, and can be redirected or scaled immediately if it underperforms. Offline media cannot.

5. Team Size Looks Adequate on Paper — Real Capacity Is Lower Than Headcount Suggests

Current roster (10.5–11 FTE): Director, Operations Specialist, Social Media Manager (FT), Social Media Manager (PT), Video Operator/Editor, Writer + Arabic Translator, Marketing Assistant (remote — also handles CCTV monitoring, outside marketing scope), PPC Manager (remote), SEO Specialist (remote), Website Developer (PT, remote), Designer (remote), Russian Translator (remote).

This headcount is in line with general industry benchmarks for a company this revenue size (companies at $10-50M revenue typically run 8-15 marketers). But two things reduce real marketing capacity below what the headcount implies:

  • At least one role is doing meaningfully non-marketing work (CCTV monitoring) inside a marketing FTE slot.
  • The Director (with AI/analytics support) personally covers work that would normally sit in separate departments: IT/equipment support, custom internal software (see Section 6), BI/analytics work, and day-to-day coordination beyond direct team management — on top of leading 10 people.

Roles a team running 4-5 distinct businesses would normally have, that this team doesn't

  • Data/Marketing Analyst — every brand's performance reporting, campaign analysis, and the budget/growth work in this document itself is currently absorbed by the Director personally (with AI support), not a dedicated role. The group's Power BI tool (R&N, ~2 years to build) only calculates numbers clinic-wide — it doesn't analyze, and it isn't marketing-specific.
  • Brand Manager(s) for the larger brands — Modern Vet alone (flagship, most complex, highest revenue) would typically warrant a dedicated brand lead in a portfolio this size; currently its strategy, positioning, and cross-channel consistency is one more thing layered onto the Director role rather than owned by a dedicated person.
  • A second Paid Media / PPC hire — there is a PPC Manager role, but it's one remote person covering every brand and clinic across the whole portfolio. A group this size and complexity would typically split paid strategy from execution, or add a second dedicated person per major brand/vertical, rather than run all paid acquisition group-wide through a single role.
This is not a headcount-increase ask in this document — it's here to show that the current structure is already tight even before considering new initiatives like offline media. Any future budget conversation should weigh these gaps against pure paid-media increases when deciding where incremental funding creates the most value.

Custom internal software — built and maintained in-house, zero agency spend

  • At least 5 custom Slack-integrated web applications built and maintained internally (driver dispatch, lab sample transfer, patient endorsement, maintenance requests, plus supporting integrations) — comparable Dubai agency-built systems of this complexity run AED 12,000–55,000 to build, plus AED 2,200–7,500/month each to maintain.
  • Equipment self-service (computers, cameras, general IT troubleshooting) — normally a dedicated IT support role (Dubai market average ~AED 240,000/year).
The inconsistency worth naming plainly: the company already accepts that specialist work costs real money — it pays eCRE8 AED 12,000/month + Klaviyo AED 7,000/month (AED 228,000/year) for e-commerce/email support, and paid SEO Serpa AED 20,000+/month for Vienna Vet. The same standard isn't being applied when marketing's own people happen to be capable of doing equivalent or harder work themselves — that capability is being treated as free.

7. The Point for Fellow Leadership

This is not a request to increase the budget (that's a separate conversation, for a separate document). This is a request to align expectations with what AED 210,000/month and an 11-person team can realistically deliver, given the above:

  • Offline media (banners, lightboxes, signboards, mall displays, city-wide out-of-home) is not currently fundable at any meaningful scale, for any brand or clinic, without displacing spend from paid channels that are already running and measurable. This isn't a Khawaneej-specific limitation — it applies group-wide under the current structure.
  • New-client acquisition growth will remain capped until paid-media budget increases, because the growth achieved so far (AOV/retention-driven) has already captured most of what's achievable without more media spend.
  • The team is already running above market efficiency (SEO, custom software, IT self-service) — "just do more with the same budget" is not a credible ask; the team is not under-delivering relative to spend, it's over-delivering, and there is a ceiling to how much further that can stretch before quality or headcount capacity breaks.
  • The department's cost-to-revenue ratio has gotten thinner as the company has grown, not proportionally larger — 2.91% of the corrected 2026 revenue prognosis (~AED 86.5M), against a vet-industry established-practice norm of 2-5% and a growth-stage/general-SMB norm of 7-10%.

8. What Comes Next (Separate Conversation)

Once this reality check has been shared and expectations are aligned, a follow-up budget proposal can be brought forward with specific, costed tiers (e.g., +1% of revenue for targeted relief/paid-media increase vs. +2% for a fuller growth-tier build-out) — but that ask will land far better once leadership already understands, from this document, exactly what the current number buys and why it cannot stretch to cover offline media or major new-client acquisition pushes as-is.
