2The Rotation Engine — How Shifts Actually Rotate

Every team-based clinic runs on one of two fixed master templates found in the sheet.

Shift blocks

  • AM ≈ 07:30–15:30 (vets) / 07:00–16:30 (nurses)
  • MID ≈ 10:00–18:00 (vets) / 09:00–18:30 (nurses)
  • PM ≈ 14:00–22:00 (vets) / 13:00–22:30 or 00:30 (nurses; later at JVC/Palm)

4-team / 4-week master pattern (Al Wasl, Palm, JVC, BOVC)

TeamMonTueWedThuFriSatSun
Team A (Wk1)offAMAMAMAMoffoff
Team B (Wk1)PMPMPMoffMIDAMAM
Team C (Wk1)MIDMIDMIDMIDoffoffMID
Team D (Wk1)AMoffoffPMPMPMPM

Each team keeps the same days on/off — only the shift type rotates AM→MID→PM→(repeat) as the week number advances. Full cycle = 4 weeks. Net result: every team works 4 consecutive days on, then 3 off, every week.

3-team / 3-week pattern (JLT, DT, Vienna)

Identical logic, 3 teams instead of 4, cycle completes in 3 weeks. Same 4-on/3-off rule per person — but with less overlap headroom, which is exactly why these clinics sit in the "needs backfill" tier: losing one team here drops coverage much further than losing one team at a 4-team clinic.

⚠️ Saturday is structurally the thinnest day, everywhere

Confirmed independently by the rotation pattern and a prior ezyVet demand analysis (2026-08-18) — Saturday has the lowest vet headcount of the week at every multi-team clinic. Any coverage-gap tool should treat Saturday as the highest-scrutiny day by default.

2-team, 7-day clinics (Azizi, Khawaneej) — different logic

Too small for AM/MID/PM rotation — single MID shift, ~7 days/week with 1-2 fixed closed days, one vet + one nurse. Any absence = an uncovered day unless externally backfilled.

10Recommendations BETA v0.1 — draft for discussion

Hypotheses built from the rotation mechanics, ezyVet demand data, configured floors, and gaps found in the current app. Not yet validated with the scheduling team — treat as a discussion starter.

A. How to optimize the schedule itself

A1. Attack Saturday structurally, not reactively

Saturday is the thinnest day at every multi-team clinic — by rotation design and by confirmed ezyVet demand data. Options: re-stagger the master templates so off-blocks spread across the week; a small dedicated Saturday floater pool; or raise minSaturday floors to match real demand instead of mirroring weekday minimums.

A2. Set floors from demand, not symmetry

Current app floors are mostly flat across weekdays. ezyVet shows real demand varies by weekday and clinic. Recompute per-clinic per-weekday floors from actual appointment volumes (e.g. 90-day rolling average) — some "shortages" today are probably phantom, and some "OK" days are actually understaffed.

A3. Formalize the summer skeleton window as data, not tribal knowledge

The summer logic (Palm skeleton, Dusko/Clara promoted, Rahaf redeployed, ~1 Jul–15 Sep) exists only in Mansoor's June doc — the app's seasonal-rules table is literally empty. Write it in as configurable date-ranged rules so it survives staff turnover and can be tuned yearly.

A4. De-risk fragile clinics (Azizi, Khawaneej) with pre-planned backup pairs

1 vet/day means any absence = closure risk. Pre-assign a named backup per fragile clinic per quarter (the app's scheduled-moves feature already half-supports this — Kurt's Azizi stints prove the pattern). Make it systematic instead of reactive.

A5. Night teams are single points of failure

Every night team is 2 people alternating 7-on/7-off — one resignation or long sickness is an instant crisis (already happened at BOVC: the Faten gap). Certify at least one more FANR-eligible night backstop per site and keep the backstop list in the app, not just in a doc.

A6. Smooth leave demand with published monthly capacity

Compute per clinic+role+month how many concurrent leave-days the floors can absorb, and publish it ("JLT vets: 2 slots in November, 0 in December"). Staff self-select into open windows instead of piling into high season and getting rejected.

A7. Close the known ultrasound Saturday gap

Prior analysis found Ivan/Isabel's rotations leave ~13 uncovered ultrasound Saturdays per year. A one-week phase shift in one rotation eliminates most of it — cheap, already-quantified win.

B. How to optimize the app's functionality

B1. Employee-facing "Can I take leave?" traffic-light calendar

Show each employee, for their own clinic+role, a green/amber/red calendar — green = floors hold even with your leave, amber = possible but needs a cover move, red = high season / floor breach. Computed from floors + approved leave + rotation (all data the app already has). Kills the ask-and-get-rejected cycle before a request is ever submitted.

B2. High-season throttling via the (currently empty) seasonal-rules engine

Let ops define date-ranged rules like "Dec 15–Jan 10: max 1 concurrent vet leave per clinic, 0 at fragile clinics" and have the calendar + approvals enforce them automatically. The engine slot exists — it's just unconfigured.

B3. Combined / split substitution solver (2-3 people covering one leave)

Current pooling moves one person to cover one absence. Real life is more flexible: Dusko covers Mon-Tue, Clara Wed, Rahaf Thu-Fri. For each day of a requested leave, walk the cover ladder independently, then assemble the cheapest combination — minimizing (a) number of distinct subs, (b) total movement cost, (c) never breaching source-clinic floors. This one feature unlocks approvals that look impossible under 1:1 substitution.

B4. Auto-suggest a cover plan on every leave application

When a request lands, attach immediately: "approvable as-is (floors hold)" / "approvable with this cover plan: [named people, days]" / "not approvable: [reason]". Every decision becomes a one-click review instead of manual sheet cross-referencing.

B5. Auto-approve the safe ones

If floors hold on every requested day with zero movement needed — approve instantly, notify the manager. Reserve human attention for requests that need judgment. (The core idea of the original Leave-Sub Finder spec — still the highest-leverage automation.)

B6. Fix the Shortages page and surface floors transparently

It currently crashes (JS error). When rebuilt: show per clinic/role/day the configured floor, scheduled headcount, approved leave, and net margin — the same math the engine uses, visible to humans.

B7. Data-hygiene guardrails

Auto-clear poolable on departure (Ramona Jazopolo is still a priority-2 cover body despite leaving in July); flag employees with no Bayzat sync (Dieter); weekly validation report. Garbage config in = garbage recommendations out.

B8. Cover-burden fairness tracking

Log every cover move per person, show running totals. The ladder always picks the same people first (Rahaf, Ehab, Kurt) — without visibility the flexible people quietly burn out. Add a soft fairness weight so repeated pulls rotate when options are equivalent.

B9. What-if simulator for the scheduling team

Preview the resulting coverage picture for affected weeks (including knock-on effects at the source clinic) before approving any leave or move.

B10. Show Bayzat leave balances at decision time

The app already syncs employee profiles from Bayzat — pull remaining entitlement too, so approvers see "requesting 10 days, has 12 left" inline.

Suggested build order (if extending the existing app)

  1. B7 data hygiene — cheap, everything else depends on clean data
  2. B1 traffic-light calendar — biggest visible win for staff, uses existing data
  3. B4 + B5 cover suggestions & auto-approve — biggest ops time-saver
  4. B3 split-substitution solver — unlocks the hard cases
  5. B2 seasonal rules + A3 summer window — config, not code, once the engine works
  6. B8/B9/B10 — quality-of-life layers on top

C. The future app — vision for a system that's maximally useful to the company

B1-B10 patch the current tool. This is the bigger question: what should the next system be, so scheduling stops being admin overhead and becomes a business advantage?

⭐ North star

The schedule is the company's supply of clinical capacity; ezyVet is the record of demand. Today they live in different systems, matched by hand in a spreadsheet. The future app's whole job: match supply to demand automatically, at minimum cost, without burning people out. Everything below serves that.

C1. One system of record — retire the sheet

Rotation templates, rosters, and assignments live in the app; the sheet becomes an auto-generated export, never hand-edited. Every discrepancy found in this hub (stale D2 roster, departed staff still listed, Dusko missing from the org chart) exists because two half-maintained systems drift. One writable system ends that class of problem forever.

C2. Demand-aware staffing (biggest untapped win)

Pull ezyVet appointment volumes + revenue into the app: floors become dynamic (computed from forecast demand), overstaffed days become visible (cost saving), understaffed days get flagged weeks ahead (revenue protection). The 2026-08-18 analysis proved the data supports this. This is the difference between a leave tracker and a capacity-planning system.

C3. Employee self-service, mobile-first

324 staff, mostly not at desks. Phone view: my shifts, my leave balance, the traffic-light calendar, one-tap requests. Plus a shift-swap marketplace: same-role colleagues agree a swap, app validates floors/rules automatically, manager one-tap confirms. Swaps today are WhatsApp folklore — formalize them.

C4. Every automated decision explains itself

Approvals, rejections, cover suggestions always show why: which floor, which rule, which alternatives were discarded. Explainability is what makes managers trust automation enough to let it run — a black box gets overridden back into manual chaos within a month. Overrides require a typed reason and are logged.

C5. Slack-native workflows

The company already runs on Slack (driver dispatch, lab transfers, endorsements — proven pattern). Leave requests, approval buttons, "you're covering Azizi Thursday" notifications land in Slack. The web app is for planning views; the daily pulse belongs where people already look.

C6. Scenario planning — a digital twin of the network

Simulate before committing: new clinic in Q2 — which teams can seed it? X resigns — where does the rotation break? Ramadan hours? 3 vets at a conference the same week? Today each question is days of spreadsheet archaeology; with rotation + floors + demand in one place, each is a button.

C7. Recruitment driven by data, not vibes

When a clinic/role breaches its floor repeatedly over N weeks (or forecast demand outgrows capacity), the app auto-raises a recruitment need with evidence attached — dates, shortfall counts, revenue at risk. Leadership sees a prioritized, justified hiring queue.

C8. Wellbeing & compliance guardrails

Track per-person: consecutive high-load weeks, night stretches, cover pulls absorbed, leave not taken (liability + burnout signal). Alert before people break, not after they resign. Encode UAE labour-law limits as hard solver constraints — compliance becomes automatic.

C9. Multi-brand, acquisition-ready

MV, BOVC, Vienna, TVRC today; the group will acquire again. Rules, floors, and templates configurable per entity, one consolidated leadership view. Onboarding an acquired clinic = "add location, set floors, import staff from Bayzat" — a day of config, not a rebuild.

C10. A small KPI layer for leadership

Coverage % by clinic/week, cover moves + cost, understaffed days prevented, leave liability trend, forecast-vs-actual accuracy. Five numbers on one page — enough for the CEO to see whether the system works without reading rosters.

Recommended path (pragmatic, low-risk)

  1. Phase 0 — decide: extend vs. rebuild. The current app's data model is good (floors, pooling rules, Bayzat sync — §11 proves it); its UX is rusty and pages are broken. Pragmatic answer: keep the API/data layer, rebuild the experience incrementally on top — don't throw away a working Bayzat sync.
  2. Phase 1 — read-only intelligence (zero risk): traffic-light calendar (B1), cover suggestions (B4), Shortages rebuilt (B6) — views on the existing API. Nothing writes; trust builds.
  3. Phase 2 — write actions: auto-approve (B5), split-substitution solver (B3), seasonal rules (B2), Slack workflows (C5).
  4. Phase 3 — intelligence: ezyVet demand model (C2), scenario planning (C6), recruitment signals (C7), wellbeing guardrails (C8).
  5. Throughout — C1 migration: each phase moves one more thing out of the sheet until the sheet is export-only.

The one-sentence pitch: stop tracking the schedule — start optimizing it.
