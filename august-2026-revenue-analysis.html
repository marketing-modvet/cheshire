Why Is August Revenue Down? — Pet Count vs. Revenue-Per-Pet vs. Vet Availability

Data through August 14, 2026 (13 full days of August invoiced: Aug 1–13) · Compared against July 2026 full month, daily-normalized

✅ Verdict: Not a demand problem — vet capacity + average cheque

Pet count per day (Counter A) is still up in August — most individual clinics show more pets/day than July, so marketing is still filling the funnel. Company-wide Counter B is roughly flat, which is worth reconciling (see note below), but neither counter shows the kind of collapse the raw revenue number implies.

The revenue shortfall comes from two things happening at once:

1. Fewer vet-days available. Active Days (doctor shifts) per day is down company-wide (33.3/day in August vs 37.7/day in July, -11.7%), and down 13-22% at most individual clinics (JVC, Downtown, The Vet Referral Centre, Khawaneej, BOVC DIP, Palm) — this directly caps how much revenue can be generated regardless of how many pets want to be seen.

2. Average cheque (Revenue Per Pet) is down sharply almost everywhere — company-wide RPP is down ~24% vs July's rate, and double digits at nearly every clinic. This is the single biggest lever on the revenue gap.

This is the clearest read yet: more individual pets are coming in, but each one is being seen fewer times on average — August's 1.185 visits/pet is not just below July, it's the lowest of the last 13 months (prior range: 1.27–1.39). Combined with RPP down -23.7%, the pattern points to a lighter, simpler case mix: more single-visit/routine cases (vaccines, basic consults) diluting the average, and fewer multi-visit/complex cases (surgery follow-ups, hospitalization, recurring treatment) that historically drove both repeat visits and higher revenue per pet.

What This Means For Marketing

The funnel still looks healthy on a per-day basis — pet count per day (Counter A) is up at most clinics, in some cases sharply (Khawaneej +68%, Hospital +18%, Palm +17%, JVC +11%). If anything, demand is at least keeping pace with, and in several clinics outpacing, the clinics' ability to convert it into revenue, because:

  • Vet-day availability is down company-wide (-11.7%/day) and down 13-22% at most individual clinics — Vienna Vet and DMCC-JLT are the only ones roughly flat
  • Revenue per pet has fallen across nearly every clinic — worth investigating whether this is case-mix (more routine/lower-value visits absorbing available vet time instead of higher-value procedures), pricing, or partial-month invoice-posting lag on big-ticket items (labs, surgery follow-ups) that haven't been billed yet

Recommended next step: re-pull this report after full month-end close to confirm the RPP gap holds (some of it may close naturally as late-invoiced high-ticket items post), and get an Ops/Scheduling view on why vet-days are down — is it planned leave, hiring gaps, or scheduling?
